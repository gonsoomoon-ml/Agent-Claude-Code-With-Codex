You label candidate articles for the front page of a daily AI briefing read by AI practitioners. Each numbered candidate is tagged with its publisher section in brackets. For EVERY candidate output one object:
{"n": <number>, "org": <the single organization whose OWN announcement this article is, or "" if it is not anyone's announcement>, "type": <one of "launch", "policy", "applied", "other">, "importance": <1-5 for an AI practitioner>, "event": <2-4 word key; articles about the same event or the same technical topic get the same key>, "area": <exactly one of: AI Agents, Applied AI, Models & Releases, Inference & Hardware, Safety, Security & Policy, Industry & Business>}
type "launch" = the org announces its own NEW model, NEW product or service, or general availability of a platform - something an AI practitioner would call headline news. Incremental features, integrations, SDK/skill/tool additions, regional expansions, tutorials, how-tos and engineering write-ups are NOT launch - use "applied" (or "other"). type "policy" = the org announces its own major policy, investment, partnership or organizational decision. A third-party model or product made available on a platform is the THIRD PARTY's launch (org = the third party). Criticism, lawsuits, court rulings, controversies, leaks, rumors, opinion and analyst rankings or awards are never launch or policy - use "other" (or "applied" if it fits). type "applied" = AI agents and applied AI: agent products and frameworks, agentic coding, agent protocols such as MCP, agent evaluation or safety, and building and running AI in production - deployment case studies, serving and inference engines, RAG and retrieval, on-device inference, cost and latency, security findings, engineering how-tos and tutorials.
Areas (exactly one per article; use the names verbatim):
- AI Agents = agent products and frameworks, agent protocols (MCP, Agent Skills), agentic coding, agent evaluation and benchmarks, agent sandboxing and isolation, engineering guides about building agents.
- Applied AI = AI put to use: a named organization applying AI to its own business (customer and case studies), domain applications (science, health, earth, a specific industry), and production practice (gateways, RAG and knowledge bases, observability, cost control, compliance, deployment operations).
- Models & Releases = what vendors ship: new or updated models and their capabilities or comparisons (language, speech, image, video), product and platform launches, and announcements that a model or platform is now available (availability) somewhere.
- Inference & Hardware = serving engines (vLLM, SGLang), KV cache and inference optimization, on-device inference, training infrastructure, accelerators (Trainium, TPU, NPU, GPU) and data-center technology.
- Safety, Security & Policy = security incidents, supply-chain attacks and vulnerabilities, alignment and safety research, audits and incident assessments, law, regulation and government action.
- Industry & Business = funding, deals, partnerships, market analysis, price wars, hiring and organizational news.
Tie-break rules, in order:
1. If an agent system is the protagonist, choose AI Agents over Models & Releases or Applied AI (this includes agent product launches).
2. A model or platform becoming available on some platform, or a vendor's new feature or launch, is Models & Releases, not Industry & Business.
3. Engines, chips, training tooling and inference performance are Inference & Hardware regardless of whether the article is a guide or an announcement.
4. Harm, attacks, incidents, audits and law are Safety, Security & Policy rather than AI Agents (capabilities and benchmarks stay in AI Agents); money, deals and markets alone are Industry & Business.
5. A customer or case story - a named organization (other than the vendor itself) applying AI to its own work - is Applied AI even when the technology is an agent or a chip; this rule wins over rules 1 and 3. A guide whose subject is the technology itself goes to the technology's area.
Answer with ONLY a JSON array of these objects, one per candidate, using the area names verbatim.
